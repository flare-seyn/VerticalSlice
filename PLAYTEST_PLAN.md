# Playtest Plan

## Research questions
- Do players understand that relics are required before the lever opens the gate?
- Which levels cause the most deaths, damage, or resets?
- Do players use dash intentionally or forget it exists?
- Are enemies read as avoidable/stompable threats?
- Is the final level difficult in a fair way?
- Does the completion summary help players understand their run?

## Player tasks
1. Start a new run and complete as many levels as possible without external help.
2. Explain what you think the relics, lever, and gate do.
3. Use dash at least once in a route where it seems helpful.
4. Try to stomp or avoid an enemy.
5. Complete the final level if possible.
6. Review the run summary and explain what it tells you.

## Quantitative metrics
- Completion time per run.
- Time per level.
- Deaths per level.
- Damage taken by source.
- Manual resets.
- Dash uses.
- Dash orb pickups.
- Relics collected.
- Enemies stomped.
- Gate locked interactions.
- Levels completed.

## Interview questions
- What was your main goal in each level?
- When did you feel confused?
- Which obstacle felt most unfair, if any?
- Did dash feel necessary, optional, or unclear?
- Did enemy behavior make sense?
- What would you change first?
- Did the final win condition feel complete?

## Observation checklist
- Player reads controls before starting.
- Player notices first relic.
- Player attempts lever before collecting all relics.
- Player understands locked gate feedback.
- Player uses dash without prompting.
- Player recognizes invulnerability/heart feedback.
- Player waits for enemy/hazard timing.
- Player gets stuck in any level for more than two minutes.
- Player understands the completion summary.

## Criteria for deciding whether the design works
- Most players can explain the relic-lever-gate loop after the first level.
- Deaths cluster around intended challenge moments, not unclear rules.
- Dash is used by players without repeated prompting.
- Players can identify why they failed after damage/death.
- Final completion feels earned rather than random.
- Telemetry matches observation notes closely enough to guide iteration.
