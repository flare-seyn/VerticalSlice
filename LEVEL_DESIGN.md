# Level Design — Current Flow and Recommended Redesign Direction

## Level flow
The current game uses six short chambers connected by relic-gated exits. The intended flow is: orient, collect relics, return to lever, unlock gate, exit.

## Teaching sequence
1. Teach movement and jump through safe ground and small gaps.
2. Introduce relics as required objectives.
3. Introduce the lever as the action that converts objective completion into gate access.
4. Introduce enemy patrols as timing threats.
5. Add traversal tools: dash, bounce pads, moving platforms, crumble platforms, and dash-refresh orbs.
6. Combine enemies and hazards in later rooms.

## Challenge escalation
- Escalate one primary concept at a time when possible.
- Avoid placing new mechanics and severe punishment in the same first encounter.
- Use relic placement to pull players through optional-looking routes while still supporting the main loop.
- Use enemy patrol bounds to define readable danger zones.

## Rest areas
Rest areas should exist near:
- Each level start.
- Before first use of a newly introduced mechanic.
- Near levers after difficult collection routes.
- Before the final gate or final hazard chain.

## Final challenge
The final challenge should synthesize learned skills rather than introduce a completely new rule. Recommended final-room beats:
1. Safe start with visible relic/gate/lever goal.
2. First timing challenge with a known hazard.
3. Enemy patrol zone that can be waited out or stomped.
4. Dash or bounce route to the last relic.
5. Return path under moderate pressure.
6. Lever unlock and clear gate.

## Intended player decisions
- Take a safer slower route or use dash for speed.
- Stomp an enemy for breathing room or avoid it to save time.
- Collect the nearest relic first or plan a route that reduces backtracking.
- Wait for moving hazards or commit to a risky timing window.
- Reset manually after a poor start or continue to recover.

## Redesigned/expanded level recommendation
For the next implementation phase, redesign one late level as a documented encounter-design showcase. The best candidate is the final level because it already contains the win condition and can demonstrate full-loop mastery. Keep the visuals and mechanics, but restructure relic placement, rest spacing, and hazard timing around a clear teach-practice-master sequence.
